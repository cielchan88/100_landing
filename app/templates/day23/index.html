Day 22 ran two monetary systems behind closed borders. Open them — let the exchange rate float and capital cross — and the sharpest differences appear.

New here? Start with the closed-economy version → /day-22/riba-and-risk
A stylized teaching model, not a forecast and not a verdict. Open-economy comparison is where it's easiest to cheat — especially by making the interest-free system look immune to capital flight. It isn't. Both systems face real trade-offs under a floating exchange rate and the impossible trinity. Treat the curves as intuition about mechanisms, not predictions about any country.

Model assumptions (click to expand)

Floating exchange-rate regime only. The model does not simulate pegs, managed floats, or capital controls — and so does not simulate regime choice under the impossible trinity. Capital flows follow an uncovered-interest-parity intuition (chasing return differentials) scaled by capital mobility; the Islamic side's response is dampened by an equity-stickiness factor, and a fixed share of any capital-flow shock still hits it regardless. The current account is a simplified function of the exchange rate and the output gap (no proper J-curve).

The conventional side adds an FX-augmented Taylor rule (rates rise to defend the currency under depreciation) and a balance-sheet output drag on sharp depreciation, representing the FX-debt fragility. The Islamic side carries a smaller symmetric depreciation drag — equity financing dodges the balance-sheet spiral, but real purchasing-power loss still hits both. Inflation persistence is anchored to target on both sides so the Taylor rule isn't fighting a phantom deflation between shocks. Cost-push inflation (supply shocks) passes through unchanged to the Islamic side — profit-and-loss sharing absorbs financial losses, not the price level.

World variables are exogenous (no foreign feedback). There is no banking-sector microstructure, no foreign-reserve management, no heterogeneity across agents. Real Islamic finance is dual-banking, full of instruments that mimic debt, and operates under FX regimes the model doesn't represent. Every simplification here is something a real model would handle with far more care. The model is built to show mechanism, not to forecast.
